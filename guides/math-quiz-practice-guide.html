Maths Quiz Practice: Questions and Shortcuts

Why Speed Maths Matters

In NTS, PPSC and CSS screening tests, the maths section is often the difference between passing and failing. The questions are rarely hard — they test speed and accuracy under time pressure. A candidate who can find 15% of 240 in seconds has a real advantage over one who reaches for long division. The good news is that speed maths is a skill, not a talent: it comes from practising a small set of patterns until they are automatic. This guide covers the patterns that appear most often.

Shortcuts You Will Use Every Day

Percentages: to find 10% of any number, move the decimal point one place left; 10% of 240 is 24, so 15% is 24 + 12 = 36. Squares: (a+b)² = a² + 2ab + b², so 102² = 10000 + 400 + 4 = 10,404. Averages: the average of consecutive numbers is the middle number — the average of 11 to 19 is 15. Multiplication by 11: for 23 × 11, add the digits (2+3=5) and put the sum between them, giving 253. Division checks: a number is divisible by 3 if its digits add to a multiple of 3, and by 9 if they add to a multiple of 9. Practise each shortcut with twenty examples until it feels easy.

The Topics That Appear Most

Expect questions on percentages, profit and loss, simple and compound interest, ratio and proportion, averages, age problems, speed-distance-time, and number series. Age problems follow one rule: set up the present ages and add or subtract the years carefully. Speed problems follow distance = speed × time. Number series usually hide a simple pattern — add 3, multiply by 2, or squares and cubes. In the exam, solve the easy questions first and mark the hard ones for later; a correct easy answer is worth exactly the same as a correct hard one.

A 15-Minute Daily Practice Routine

Set a timer for 15 minutes. Do ten mixed questions without a calculator, then check your answers and write down every mistake with the correct method. Keep a small book of formulas and review it for two minutes before each session. Once a week, do a timed 20-question test in 10 minutes — the timer tool on this site is made for exactly that. Track your score in the Score Tracker and watch your average climb. Within a month, most learners double their speed on the common question types.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is 15% of 240?
A: 36. Ten percent is 24 and five percent is 12, so 15% is 24 + 12 = 36.

Q: How do I get faster at maths quizzes?
A: Practise a few shortcuts daily without a calculator, and always work under a timer so exam pressure feels familiar.

Q: Which maths topics matter most for NTS and PPSC?
A: Percentages, averages, ratio and proportion, age problems, and speed-distance-time appear in almost every paper.
