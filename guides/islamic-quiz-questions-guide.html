Islamic Quiz Questions: Guide and Practice

What Islamic Quizzes Cover

Islamic quiz competitions in Pakistan usually cover the Quran, the life of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), the companions, Islamic history, worship and basic beliefs. Typical questions ask about the number of Surahs in the Quran (114), the first revealed word ('Iqra', meaning 'Read'), the first Surah revealed (Al-Alaq), the longest Surah (Al-Baqarah) and the shortest Surah (Al-Kawthar). Other common areas are the five pillars of Islam, the months of the Islamic calendar, the major battles such as Badr and Uhud, and the rightly guided caliphs. School and college competitions usually keep questions at this foundational level.

Learning With Respect and Accuracy

Islamic knowledge deserves careful, respectful study. Use authentic sources: a good translation of the Quran with commentary, reliable books of Seerah (the Prophet's biography) and your school or college Islamiat textbook. Be careful with facts taken from social media, where quotes are often misattributed and dates are sometimes wrong. If a question involves a verse or a hadith, check the reference — the Surah name and number, or the hadith collection — before accepting it. For children, keep learning gentle and story-based: the life of the Prophet, the stories of the prophets and the meaning of daily prayers are the best starting point.

A Simple Study Plan

Divide the subject into five parts and study one per week: the Quran, the Seerah, the companions and caliphs, worship and pillars, and Islamic history after the caliphate. Each day, read for 20 minutes and write down ten facts as questions. Test yourself or a family member at the end of the week. Mosque study circles and school quiz teams are excellent for practice because they turn revision into a friendly competition. Keep a small notebook of mistakes and review it before any competition.

Preparing for a Competition

In the last week before a quiz competition, revise your notebook rather than starting new books. Practise answering quickly, because most competitions reward speed as well as accuracy. Learn the exact wording of famous facts, such as the number of verses in commonly asked Surahs, and double-check any number you are unsure about. On the day, stay calm, listen to the full question before answering, and do not guess wildly on negative-marking rounds. Remember that the real goal is learning, and a competition is only one way to measure it.

Practice Questions to Try

Test yourself with these questions, then check your answers in a reliable source. How many Surahs are in the Quran? (114.) Which Surah is the longest? (Al-Baqarah.) Which is the shortest? (Al-Kawthar.) What was the first word revealed? ('Iqra', meaning 'Read'.) Name the five pillars of Islam: Shahadah (faith), Salah (prayer), Zakat (charity), Sawm (fasting) and Hajj (pilgrimage). Who was the first caliph after the Prophet (peace be upon him)? (Hazrat Abu Bakr.) In which month do Muslims fast? (Ramadan.) Which city is the birthplace of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him)? (Makkah.) How many months are in the Islamic calendar? (Twelve.) Which battle was the first major battle in Islamic history? (Badr, in 2 AH.) If you answered eight or more correctly, you are ready for a school-level competition; if not, revise the Quran and Seerah sections of this guide and try again in a week.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What are the most common Islamic quiz questions?
A: Questions about the Quran (number of Surahs, first and last revelations), the five pillars, the Seerah and the rightly guided caliphs appear most often.

Q: Which books are best for Islamic quiz preparation?
A: A Quran translation with commentary, a standard Seerah book, and your Islamiat textbook are the safest starting points.

Q: Are Islamic quiz questions asked in exams too?
A: Yes. Islamiat is a compulsory subject in Pakistani schools and a paper in CSS and other competitive exams.
