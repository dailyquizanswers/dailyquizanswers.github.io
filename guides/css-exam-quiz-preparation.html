CSS Exam Quiz Preparation Guide

What the CSS Exam Is

The Central Superior Services (CSS) exam is Pakistan's most prestigious competitive examination, conducted by the Federal Public Service Commission (FPSC). It selects officers for the federal civil service — the Pakistan Administrative Service, Police Service, Foreign Service, Customs, Income Tax and other groups. The exam has three stages: a written examination, a medical and psychological assessment, and an interview. The written exam itself has compulsory subjects worth 600 marks and optional subjects worth 600 marks, making 1,200 marks in total. Candidates must also pass an MPT screening test before they can sit the written exam.

Compulsory Subjects and the Screening Test

The compulsory subjects are English Essay, English (Precis and Composition), General Science and Ability, Current Affairs, Pakistan Affairs and Islamic Studies (or Comparative Study of Major Religions for non-Muslim candidates). The MPT (MCQ-based Preliminary Test) has 200 multiple-choice questions covering English, general abilities, Pakistan Affairs, Islamic Studies, current affairs, everyday science, Urdu and basic mathematics. It is a qualifying test: you must pass it to reach the written exam, and its marks do not count towards the final result. Because it is entirely MCQ-based, quiz-style practice is the perfect preparation for it.

A Realistic Preparation Plan

Most successful candidates prepare for eight to twelve months. Start with the compulsory subjects, because they decide the result, and choose optional subjects you already know well or can score in — high-scoring subjects like Political Science, International Relations, History and Geography are popular for a reason. Read one English newspaper daily, keep current affairs notes month by month, and write one essay every week from the second month onwards. Practise past papers under timed conditions, and attempt the MPT pattern with timed MCQ tests — the timer tool on this site matches the screening test's pressure.

Mistakes That Cost Candidates the Exam

The most expensive mistake is poor English: the essay and precis papers have high failure rates, so English must be practised daily, not left to the end. The second mistake is choosing optional subjects because a friend chose them rather than because you can score in them. The third is ignoring Pakistan Affairs and Islamiat, which are high-scoring and predictable. The fourth is studying without a timetable and without weekly self-testing. Finally, always check the official FPSC website for the current syllabus, exam dates and eligibility rules — these change, and only the official source is authoritative.

A Sample Weekly Timetable

A workable CSS week looks like this. Monday to Friday: two hours of optional subjects, one hour of Pakistan Affairs or Current Affairs, one hour of English (alternate essay and precis), and 30 minutes of MCQ practice with a timer. Saturday: write one full essay, revise the week's current affairs notes, and attempt a past paper question. Sunday: revise the mistake notebook, read one newspaper editorial closely, and rest — rest is part of preparation. Each month, attempt one full past paper under exam conditions. Adjust the hours to your life, but keep the structure: daily English, daily current affairs, weekly essay, weekly timed test. Candidates who follow a written timetable for six months arrive at the exam hall calm, because nothing in the paper is a surprise.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Who conducts the CSS exam in Pakistan?
A: The Federal Public Service Commission (FPSC).

Q: What is the MPT in the CSS exam?
A: The MCQ-based Preliminary Test — a 200-question screening test you must pass before sitting the written exam.

Q: How long should CSS preparation take?
A: Most candidates prepare for 8 to 12 months with a daily study routine. Check the official FPSC website for current dates and rules.
